Information theory · Statistics · Machine learning

Mathematical foundations
of reliable AI.

I study what information AI systems need to learn and make decisions—and what evidence is sufficient to assess their reliability.

My research connects information theory, statistics and machine learning to understand learned representations, detect model failures, and evaluate systems under limited evidence. My emerging programme extends these foundations to adaptive and collaborative AI.

Three connected questions

Research programme, 2027–2031
01

What information makes a model useful?

Mathematical foundations of representations, learning and privacy, grounded in downstream decisions.

02

What evidence supports reliability?

Evaluation, uncertainty and failure prediction, extending towards adaptive testing of models and agents.

03

When should AI systems collaborate?

Information acquisition, pragmatic communication and decision-making under resource constraints.

Research across institutions and disciplines

Since 2022, I have directed ILLS in Montréal, shaping its scientific programme and developing France–Canada research and training partnerships. The laboratory brings together CNRS, CentraleSupélec, Université Paris-Saclay, McGill, ÉTS and Mila.

My academic and industry collaborations connect foundational questions with language, medical imaging, molecular discovery and engineering applications.
