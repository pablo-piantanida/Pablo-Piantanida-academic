Research

Information, evaluation
and intelligent action.

How can an AI system represent, evaluate and exchange information reliably when evidence, computation and communication are limited?

I combine fundamental limits and mathematical characterisations with estimators, algorithms and experiments. Each direction connects a precise scientific question to a method and a credible evaluation setting.

01 / Mathematical foundations

What information makes a model useful?

I study what learned representations preserve, how their usefulness depends on downstream decisions, and what statistical limits govern learning and privacy. Information theory provides operational measures of relevance, compression and leakage; statistical decision theory makes model comparisons meaningful for a task.

Established contributions

  • Information Maximization for Few-Shot Learning · NeurIPS 2020
  • A Differential Entropy Estimator for Training Neural Networks · ICML 2022
  • When is an Embedding Model More Promising than Another? · NeurIPS 2024
  • Statistical Deficiency for Task Inclusion Estimation · ACL 2025
  • Fundamental Limits of Membership Inference Attacks on Machine Learning Models · JMLR 2025

Programme for 2027–2031

Develop finite-data comparisons of representations and learning systems through their consequences for decisions. Study how information sufficiency and statistical deficiency guide model selection and transfer, and establish limits for evaluation and privacy auditing under explicit observation and access assumptions.

02 / Evaluation and uncertainty

What evidence supports reliability?

Models can make confident errors, behave unpredictably under distribution shift, or be evaluated with imperfect metrics. I develop methods for prediction-error detection, uncertainty assessment, evaluation of generated language, and privacy and generative-system auditing.

Established contributions

  • DOCTOR · NeurIPS 2021
  • IGEOOD · ICLR 2022
  • Relative uncertainty for misclassification detection · ICLR 2024
  • Beyond the Norms: Detecting Prediction Errors in Regression Models · ICML 2024
  • InfoLM · AAAI 2022 · Outstanding Student Paper
  • COSMIC · ACL 2024
  • MOSAIC · Findings of ACL 2025

Programme for 2027–2031

Develop adaptive evaluation procedures that select informative tests and stop when enough evidence supports a specified decision. Account for question-calibration uncertainty, imperfect automated judges and limited human feedback. Validation will examine model comparison, performance regressions and multi-step agent behaviour, with explicit assumptions for any finite-sample risk guarantees.

Related project: REGARD

03 / Adaptive and collaborative AI

When is collaboration worth its cost?

Decision-makers often have partial observations and different information. My work studies how communication and compression support cooperation, from interactive source coding to pragmatic reasoning in dialogue.

Established contributions

  • The Three-Terminal Interactive Lossy Source Coding · IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 2017
  • Collaborative Information Bottleneck · IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 2019
  • Rational Retrieval Acts · SIGIR 2025
  • Collaborative Rational Speech Act · EMNLP 2025

Programme for 2027–2031

Develop policies that decide whether to compute further, acquire evidence, use a tool, communicate or request assistance. The initial focus is cooperative systems with private information, imperfect observations and mismatched beliefs. Compare collaborative policies with strong single-agent alternatives under matched computation, communication and oversight budgets.

From information theory to the foundations of AI

My work on communication networks, distributed inference, privacy and interactive source coding asks an operational question: what task can be accomplished from partial observations and limited communication? The same question guides my research on representations, evaluation and collaboration.

Information theory remains the mathematical foundation of this trajectory. Statistics and learning theory connect information measures to finite-sample inference and generalisation, while experiments on modern AI systems test their practical significance.
